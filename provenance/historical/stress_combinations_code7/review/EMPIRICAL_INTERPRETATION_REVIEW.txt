CODE7 empirical expansion: independent result review
28 October 2026

VERIFIED RESULT
The primary WT panel is unchanged from CODE6 (19,180 genes). Independent column-pivoted QR reproduces the SVD subspaces and gain decomposition. Exact unequal-library U-statistic cross-products reproduce the covariance-corrected disjoint-reference geometry. All six genotype/filter scenarios pass these numerical checks. CODE6 source files, frozen arrays, predictions and results are unchanged by hash.

The primary ranks are 3, 9 and 11. The original outside-V3 contribution to mean MSE gain is 0.06227301898595472. It partitions into the additional single-library directions, the additional control-variation directions, and the outside-V11 contribution of 0.05567352719347853. Thus 89.4023% of the original outside prediction-gain contribution remains outside the full observed lower-order library span. This is NOT a percentage of biological variance or a noise-free fraction.

The outside-V11 gains are positive for all three target RNA libraries: 0.047636669228740755, 0.05875435904068035, 0.06062955331101449. On the separate singles-only diagnostic panel, outside-V9 covariance-corrected margins are positive for all three overlapping reference splits: 0.21838127665103077, 0.21616586368671287, 0.19817155391424626.

WHAT THIS SUPPORTS
The outside label is not solely caused by restricting the observed lower-order data to its three condition means. Most of the original prediction-error contribution remains after admitting the measured within-single-condition and control variability. Neither donor nor target data selected these directions or the ranks.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT SUPPORT
V11 is the span of observed lower-order libraries, not the true biological single-response subspace. The added directions can contain technical variation and biological heterogeneity. With three RNA pools per treatment, unobserved noise directions and covariance remain uncertain. A low-dimensional observed-variation expansion cannot rule out estimation error in directions absent from those few samples. The conditional null simulations remain necessary and must be reported even if less favorable.

The disjoint companion correctly uses V3 and V9 only. Including the two held-out reference controls in its fitted subspace would invalidate the simple cross-reference covariance logic; the code rejects that option. Its margins differ from the observed prediction-gain estimand and should not be used as larger estimates of the same effect.
