Status: PASS WITH INTERPRETIVE LIMITS. No annotation or category changes made.

Scope
Read functional_summary.csv, functional_core_summary.csv, strict_core_summary.csv,
strict_core_examples.csv and the source-paper abstract. This is post-result
attribution of an already observed predictive advantage, not new external
validation or prospective biological hypothesis testing. Positive gain is lower
squared prediction error for the pair centroid than its named comparator.

Supported thread
Against pair_trained_factor_OLS, translation has positive mean gain in both
genotypes (Car 0.193779; Cleo 0.533303), with 75.40% and 85.29% mean-positive
features versus 64.94% and 70.21% in the eligible BP background. Translation is
therefore a defensible descriptive example of pair-condition information that
this fitted single-response model does not capture. Its net contribution is
only 2.07% and 3.84% of total gain; it is not the main explanation of the global
advantage. Most gain lies outside the six chosen categories, including features
without BP or any GO annotation.

Contradictions that must stay visible
The translation pattern is comparator dependent: against second_order_factorial,
Car translation has mean gain -0.013303 and only 45.99% mean-positive features
(BP background 56.54%); Cleo remains positive (0.086009; 63.49%). A uniform
translation pathway advantage across baselines is unsupported. Protein folding
also lacks a uniform story: Cleo mean gain against OLS is -0.230564 although
62.92% of folding features are positive. Harmful tail magnitude outweighs the
positive count. Counting favorable genes alone would conceal that reversal.
Protein-folding GO membership is not a direct UPR pathway annotation.

Shared strict core
The intersection across second-order factorial, OLS and within-V3 comparisons
contains 604 of 15,466 shared primary-panel features; 304 are BP eligible.
Translation contributes 23/365 common eligible features (relative prevalence
1.589 versus BP background), but folding contributes only 4/174 (0.580), and
239 of the 304 BP core features lie outside the six categories. These are
descriptive counts and prevalence ratios, not tested enrichment. No single
program explains this diverse core. Shared predictability also does not imply
shared activation: examples include MAPKKK18 (gene-LOC102619746) and the
glycine-rich cell-wall protein-like gene-LOC107174352 with opposite target
expression-change signs across genotypes. Examples were ranked by minimum
prediction-gain percentile across contexts; they are illustrations, not newly
validated causal genes or independent mechanistic evidence.

Annotation limits
All GAF annotations are IEA computational assignments from NCBI AR103, matched
by exact Entrez ID to the original AR102 source features. The GAF and GO.db
ontology both use GO 2023-07-27; BP membership propagated only via is_a/part_of.
All source Entrez-to-feature ambiguity is excluded from functional summaries.
The appropriate background is eligible BP annotation (Car 8,300; Cleo 7,883),
not all GO annotation. MF/CC-only and unannotated features remain explicit.
The six categories overlap and cannot be summed as disjoint totals.
GO:0098869, cellular oxidant detoxification, has just 10 Car and 9 Cleo primary
features and zero strict-core features. It cannot support a broad redox claim
or a stable category-level conclusion. Partial annotation and all-IEA evidence
preclude asserting validated mechanism or interpreting no annotation as no
biological function. No formal pathway-enrichment claims are justified.

Prior-art boundary
The original paper, “Omics analyses in citrus reveal a possible role of RNA
translation pathways and Unfolded Protein Response regulators in the tolerance
to combined drought, high irradiance, and heat stress” (Horticulture Research
2023, uhad107; https://doi.org/10.1093/hr/uhad107), already highlighted Carrizo
RNA metabolism/translation and ER/UPR responses, including HSPs, BiP2 and
PDIL2-2. This review checked its archived full-text XML abstract under CODE8
metadata/prior_metadata_snapshot/uhad107_fulltext.xml. Thus involvement of
translation or UPR is not the new finding. The incremental result is the
comparison-specific distribution of pair-to-triple predictive information,
including where that advantage fails. CODE9 does not resolve CODE7's noise/
identifiability limit or establish an additional true biological dimension.

Suggested claim
“Pair-condition measurements retained predictive information across diverse
functional backgrounds. Translation-associated features showed a particularly
consistent descriptive advantage over the fitted single-response OLS model,
but this pattern was not uniform across alternative comparators; no single
annotated process accounted for the global gain.”
