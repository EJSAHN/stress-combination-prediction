CONCENTRATION IS COMPARATOR-DEPENDENT
The advantage over learned single-response weights is not carried by just a few extreme features: positive mean gain occurs in65.25% of Carrizo genes and70.60% of Cleopatra genes, and gain remains positive in every target library after removing the10% largest mean contributors. To erase the mean advantage by adversarial deletion requires3,063/17,470 (17.53%) and4,035/16,247 (24.84%) features, respectively.
The outside-observed-single-span advantage is broader by these descriptive metrics: positive mean gain in71.96% and78.33%, and adversarial deletion fractions34.32% and43.02% to erase it. This remains a statement about measured spans, not true biological dimensions or independent gene counts.
The advantage over second-order factorial extrapolation is MORE concentrated. It disappears after removing772/17,470 (4.42%) Carrizo features or1,138/16,247 (7.00%) Cleopatra features. Carrizo fails the5% deletion stress test; both fail10%. It would be wrong to summarize CODE9 as 'all advantages survive10% removal' or 'no outlier sensitivity'. This outcome-selected score stress test does not invalidate CODE8 whole-panel prediction.

GLOBAL MAGNITUDE DOES NOT EXHAUST THE OBSERVED DIFFERENCE
With target-informed global rescaling, second-order factorial MSE becomes0.244452 in Carrizo and0.352849 in Cleopatra, still worse than the UNADJUSTED frozen centroid MSE0.212011 and0.279195. Thus optimizing one global scale for that comparator cannot reproduce centroid accuracy on these observed targets. The comparator's fitted alpha values0.80085 and0.75527 are outcome-seen diagnostics, not new predictors.
The centroid's own target-fitted scales are1.28176 and1.24408, reducing its MSE to0.157065 and0.225265. This shows its original magnitude is not optimal; it does not authorize replacing the fixed predictions or reporting the oracle errors as validation performance. Gene-wise or more flexible rescaling is a different, untested explanation.

SUITABLE CLAIM
The fixed pair-response observations provide broadly distributed predictive information beyond the specified single-response weighted model, with a shared subset across the two citrus genotypes. Their additional advantage over second-order factorial extrapolation is more concentrated, although even target-optimized global scaling of that extrapolation does not match the original centroid. Functional labels describe where measured prediction gains occur; causal mechanisms, true-span identifiability and formal pathway significance remain unresolved.
