SCIENTIFIC CLAIMS AND INTERPRETATION LIMITS

This release prepares the executed analysis and figures for a research paper.
It does not itself establish publication-level novelty or a causal mechanism.

CENTRAL QUESTION
How much information about a higher-order stress combination is available in
observed lower-order expression profiles, and how repeatable is the allocation
of a prediction's accuracy gain among genes?

The proposed contribution is an empirical comparison of fixed prediction rules
and the limits of their interpretation. Equal-weight averaging, weighted single-
response integration, projection, cross-repeat moments and complementary-pool
refitting are established mathematical tools. Their use here is not a claim
that a new general prediction algorithm, correlation estimator or biological
law has been invented.

EVIDENCE STRUCTURE

1. Discovery: GSE147962, Arabidopsis, four stress factors and three RNA pools per
   factorial cell. The author-processed values are DESeq2-normalized counts.
   Donor exploration and rank choices occurred after inspecting parts of this
   dataset. A selected pair of quiet triple-condition profiles predicted the
   four-way profile better than the control, but donor-selection rules did not
   generalize uniformly across triple/four-way targets. The common-response
   selector chose the same primary donors as ordinary distance to control.
   Keep these unfavorable comparisons visible. Do not market the selector as
   a superior new method or count the same four-way result more than once.

2. Separate Arabidopsis experiment: GSE330879, wild type and rap2.3_1, salt,
   excess light and heat, with the complete eight-condition design and three
   RNA pools per cell. The primary wild-type/filter test and secondary
   mutant/filter scenarios were fixed and predictions frozen before numerical
   triple-target parsing. The rule was the equal mean of all three pair-stress
   profiles. The source supplied FPKM, not raw counts. This experiment came
   from the same investigator network as discovery; it is not independent-
   laboratory replication or an exact replication of selected triples
   predicting a four-way treatment.

3. Citrus experiment: GSE203331, Carrizo citrange and Cleopatra mandarin,
   water stress, high light and heat, with three RNA pools per factorial cell.
   Each pool combines plants; the number of independent RNA pools remains
   three. Carrizo is a Poncirus trifoliata × Citrus sinensis hybrid and
   Cleopatra is Citrus reshni. Therefore 'Arabidopsis and citrus' or 'two plant
   systems' is safer than 'two species'. The source file contains FPKM, TPM and
   read-count columns; the fixed analysis uses the columns labelled FPKM.
   Predictions for both genotypes and all expression filters were frozen
   before numerical triple-target parsing. The crop and experiment differ,
   while investigators overlap with earlier work. Both genotypes were mapped
   to a common sweet-orange reference.

All analyses start from publicly deposited, author-processed expression
matrices. No raw FASTQ reprocessing was performed. Numerical target withholding
within the reanalysis does not remove possible coupling introduced by the
authors' source-wide preprocessing or published qualitative knowledge.

WHAT THE RESULTS SUPPORT

The same pair-centroid rule, fitted separately to each genotype's own lower-
order observations, achieved lower mean squared error than the specified
comparators in the fixed external Arabidopsis and citrus tests. All gene
filters, comparator definitions, transformations, sample mappings and target
libraries must remain stated. Absolute MSE values should not be compared
between normalized-count and FPKM scales as if they measured a common unit.

The centroid's observed component outside the estimated single-response span
contributes to prediction gain. Expanding that span with observed single-stress
and control replicate variation retains much of the gain. The correct phrase
is 'outside the fitted/observed response span', not 'pure higher-order
interaction', a previously absent biological dimension or causal protection.

In citrus, the centroid's advantage over learned single-response weights is
distributed across many genes and survives deleting the highest-gain 10% from
scoring. Its advantage over the second-order model is more concentrated and
does not survive the same deletion. One global target-fitted rescaling of the
second-order vector still does not reach the original centroid's error. This
rescaling is an outcome-seen diagnostic, not another validated predictor.

On the common 15,466-feature citrus universe, the centroid remains more
accurate than each of three comparators across all 1,024 sampled side-specific
refits. There are 3,072 mean-gain comparisons, not 3,072 independent biological
experiments. The models are rebuilt separately within each genotype; no model
trained only in one genotype is transferred to predict the other genotype.

Gene-gain ranks are more concordant within genotype than between genotypes.
The target-repeat correction changes between-genotype Pearson coefficients
only slightly, and the within-versus-between contrast persists when fitting
and target scoring use complementary pool sides. This supports conditional
repeatability of an observed difference in gain profiles. It does not prove
that the genotypes use different causal stress-response mechanisms.

RESULTS THAT LIMIT THE STRONGER INTERPRETATION

Observed response spans are estimated with noise. True lower-order directions
may lie outside an estimated span, while higher-order effects may lie inside.
A conditional simulation with a joint rank-three latent mean and empirical
residual covariance reaches the observed wild-type outside-span statistic:
observed 0.062273019; simulated 95th percentile 0.064190945; 56/1,000 outcomes
at least as large. The corresponding mutant count is 61/1,000. All 36 fixed
scenarios are retained. This neither proves that the gain is noise nor
establishes a biological P value. The joint latent model uses the already-seen
target as a nuisance construction; it is not a predictor. Three pools cannot
identify the full covariance or true span orientation. Matching one scalar
statistic does not demonstrate a fit to the whole expression dataset.

Gene-wise gains are differences of squared prediction error. They depend on
the comparator, scale, panel and fitted model. They are not gene effects,
causal contributions, differential expression or pathway activation.
Functional summaries are descriptive and comparator-dependent. Carrizo
translation has negative net gain against the second-order comparator;
Cleopatra protein-folding features have negative net gain against learned
single-response weights. These patterns preclude a universal beneficial
pathway claim. The original citrus study already discussed translation and
unfolded-protein responses.

The functional annotation join uses exact Entrez identifiers from a newer
NCBI annotation release, with a date-matched ontology. All source GAF evidence
is computational IEA. Ambiguous duplicated Entrez mappings are excluded from
functional summaries, not silently resolved. Categories can overlap; small
oxidant-detoxification groups contain only 10/9 primary-panel features. No GO
enrichment P values or genes-as-biological-replicates tests were calculated.

Conditional target-repeat reliability holds the fitted predictor fixed. It
does not remove errors shared through that predictor. Its near-one stable
moment fraction is not a proportion of biological truth or the reliability
of the complete refitting procedure. Adjusted Pearson is a plug-in ratio,
not an unbiased correlation estimator; the formula is not applied to ranks.

Complementary sides within a draw use disjoint pools, but different draws
reuse the same observations. The 1,024 fits are not mutually disjoint. Shared
feature selection, reference mapping and preprocessing remain. Full fitting
panels differ between citrus genotypes even though scoring uses common IDs.
The 2.5–97.5% ranges describe partition sensitivity, not confidence intervals.
All-positive matched contrasts provide no calibrated significance test.

The experiments are endpoint expression measurements. Do not infer recovery,
hysteresis, memory, resistance or tolerance from these prediction diagnostics.

RELEASE AND VALIDATION LANGUAGE

Use 'recomputed from deposited processed expression matrices', not 'raw-read
reanalysis'. Distinguish numerical replay, independent numerical audit, figure
source verification, PowerShell syntax/contract review, and actual execution
on Windows. The complete native Windows run 20261028_170808_faf6bd34 passed
on 28 October 2026. This establishes portability, not new biological validation.

The code license must not relabel third-party inputs.
Public GEO availability and source URLs should be documented without claiming
that deposited data are covered by the project's code license. NCBI's Molecular
Data Usage policy places no NCBI restrictions on use/distribution of molecular
data, including gene expression, but does not certify or transfer third-party
rights. A code repository with pinned acquisition instructions and checksums,
and a separate local reproduction bundle, makes these input roles clear.

FIGURE REVIEW CHECKLIST

Main figures should state the question/design, show fixed prediction
comparisons, show both span persistence and its noise-identifiability limit,
and distinguish aggregate accuracy from gene-profile agreement. Supplements
must retain failed discovery selection, filter/library sensitivity, the full
noise-scenario grid and comparator-dependent functional/QC results. All
panels require numerical source tables, correct sample units and traceable
links to executed outputs. No panel may turn a simulation exceedance into a
biological P value, a partition range into a confidence interval, or an
observed-span component into a factorial interaction.
